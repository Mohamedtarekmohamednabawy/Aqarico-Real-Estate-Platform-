# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Aqarico serves a mixed real estate marketplace audience: buyers, renters, individual sellers, real estate agents, agencies, developers, admins, and market explorers. Users may arrive with very different levels of confidence, from someone casually comparing neighborhoods to an agent managing active listings or an admin reviewing platform activity.

The product should support fast scanning, trustworthy decision-making, and direct action across property discovery, listing management, agent verification, saved homes, comparisons, dashboards, and account workflows.

## Product Purpose

Aqarico helps people search, evaluate, list, compare, and manage real estate with more confidence. It combines property listings, verified agents, map-based discovery, market dashboards, saved favorites, comparison tools, and seller workflows into one practical platform.

Success means users can move from uncertainty to a clear next step: contact a verified agent, compare properties, understand a market, publish a listing, manage existing inventory, or make an informed inquiry without friction.

## Brand Personality

Trusted, calm, and performance-minded.

The interface should feel capable and polished without becoming cold. It should communicate reliability, clarity, and momentum while still having enough visual character to avoid feeling generic, basic, or boring.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic real estate templates, repetitive card grids, plain orange SaaS styling, cluttered broker portals, luxury-only visual language, over-decorated dashboards, vague marketing copy, and interfaces that feel slow, noisy, or unfinished.

Avoid designs that rely on oversized decorative cards, predictable hero sections, weak hierarchy, low-contrast warm palettes, or visual flourishes that do not help users make property decisions.

## Design Principles

1. Make trust visible: verification, provenance, ownership, and contact actions should be clear at the moment users need them.
2. Prioritize decision speed: layouts should help users compare, filter, scan, and act quickly without feeling rushed.
3. Keep calm under complexity: dashboards, listing flows, and agent details should organize dense information with restraint and rhythm.
4. Give the product a point of view: avoid default marketplace patterns when a more memorable, useful structure can serve the workflow.
5. Design for mixed roles: buyers, sellers, agents, agencies, and admins should each feel that the interface understands their context.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aqarico should target WCAG AA as a baseline. Interfaces should be mobile-first, keyboard-accessible, color-blind safe, and reduced-motion friendly.

The product should be Arabic/RTL-ready as a future design and implementation consideration, even if the current interface is English-first. Text, navigation, forms, maps, tables, and dashboards should avoid assumptions that make localization difficult later.
